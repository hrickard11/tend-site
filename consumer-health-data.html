Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy

Effective and last updated September 26, 2026.

This policy is for Tend, a game for adults in the United States made by Hayden Rickard. It covers "consumer health data" as Washington's My Health My Data Act, Nevada's consumer health data law (SB 370), Connecticut's consumer health data rules and similar laws define it: information that's linked or reasonably linkable to you and identifies your past, present or future physical or mental health. We apply it to everyone who uses Tend, wherever you live. Our privacy policy covers everything else.

The consumer health data we collect

  • Steps, exercise minutes and workouts.
  • The sessions you do in Tend: time, distance, steps and mile splits (never the route).
  • Weight, height, goal weight, and whether you want to lose, keep or gain weight.
  • The food you log and its calories, and your favorite and saved foods.
  • The sets and workouts you log.
  • Rest days, and days you mark yourself sick or hurt.
  • If you turn them on: sleep and resting heart rate, read on your phone. Tend keeps only what it made of them (for example, "well rested"), not the readings.
  • With Tend+, if you open heart-rate zones: heart rate during a session, read on your phone and not kept.
  • What the game makes of all this: your pet's growth, your streaks, weeks active, milestones and tier.

We don't collect biometric data, precise location, or any data about reproductive or sexual health, gender-affirming care, or health conditions beyond what's listed here. We don't use geofencing around any place that provides health care.

Where it comes from

  • Apple Health, with your permission (including what other apps, smart scales and your Apple Watch write there).
  • Your iPhone's motion sensor and your Apple Watch, during a session.
  • What you type into Tend.

Why we collect and use it

  • To run the game: to feed and grow your pet, set fair goals, show your progress, and give treats and milestones.
  • To back your game up to your account, so it isn't lost and can come with you to a new phone.
  • For the friend features you choose, and only with your separate OK to share your activity.
  • To keep Tend working and fair, using simple usage events that never hold your numbers.

We never use it for advertising, never sell it, and never store it in iCloud.

Your consent

Tend asks for two separate consents on their own screen, before it reads or sends anything:

  1. To collect and use your health data for the purposes above. It's needed to play, since your health is what feeds your pet.
  2. To share your activity with friends. Optional, and off unless you turn it on. Asked separately, and never bundled with anything else.

Each is recorded with its date and the version of the words you agreed to, and you can see both in Settings, then Your privacy. You can withdraw either one there anytime. Withdrawing stops the collection or sharing right away; it doesn't delete what's already stored, which you can delete there too.

We don't share consumer health data with advertisers, data brokers, Google AdMob, Open-Meteo or Open Food Facts. We have no affiliates. We may disclose it if the law requires it, and we'd tell you if the law allows.

We never sell it

We don't sell consumer health data, and we never will without first getting your signed, written authorization, as Washington's law requires. We have never sold it.

Your rights

  • To confirm and access: to know whether we collect, share or sell your consumer health data, to get a copy of it, and to get a list of every third party and affiliate we've shared it with, with their contact information. In the app: Settings, then Your privacy, then Download my data. The file includes the list.
  • To delete it: Settings, then Delete my account, or write to us. We delete it from our server (and so from our server host, Supabase) right away, and from the server's backups within 30 days. What friends saw while you were sharing stops showing once it's deleted.
  • To withdraw your consent to collecting or sharing it, anytime, in Settings, then Your privacy.
  • To not be treated differently for using any of these rights.

How to make a request, and appeal

Email hrickard26@gmail.com, or use Contact us in the app. Write from your account's email, or tell us your username, so we can check it's you. We'll answer within 45 days (and if we need up to 45 more, we'll tell you why). Requests are free.

If we turn down your request, reply to our answer and ask us to look again. We'll answer your appeal within 45 days and explain our decision. If we deny it, you can contact your state attorney general. In Washington: atg.wa.gov/file-complaint. In Nevada: ag.nv.gov. In Connecticut: portal.ct.gov/ag.

How we protect it

It travels encrypted, only your account can read your backup, and only the people named above ever get any of it. If there's ever a breach, we'll tell you and the Federal Trade Commission within 60 days, sooner where a state requires it (see our privacy policy).

Changes

If we want to collect consumer health data for a new purpose, or share it in a new way, we'll ask for your consent first. The date at the top says when this page last changed.
